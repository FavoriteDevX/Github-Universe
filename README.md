# 🌐 GitHub Multiverse

<img width="1000" height="561" alt="GitHub Multiverse" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/1c7a81c7-6897-4ce4-bc3c-422fa9a61cfe" />

A Decentraland-like, fully functional 3D collaborative metaverse built for developers. Instead of clunky character avatars, every developer walks the world as a custom **three-dimensional mouse pointer**. Everyone starts in **Genesis City**, one continuous, organic space around Genesis Plaza (no street grid, no square blocks) made of small themed scenes, with an **underground level** below the plaza, a **metro** to every district, and bigger **Worlds**: interactive mini-games and a Silicon Valley tech campus.

So, imagine to have a fun, useful and 3D virtual dimension world, just for devs like you. Meet new friends, code like a pro, discover who uses the same tools that you do, invite friends to dynamic workspaces and explore your world.

---

## 🚀 Features

- **Real-Time Multiplayer Sync**: Powered by Socket.io. Every developer's movement, chat, name and wearables are replicated instantly to all connected clients.
- **3D Cursor Avatars**: Everyone is a clean, pastel-colored 3D arrow with a name tag showing the **username you chose at sign-in**. You walk, run and jump in third person, like in Decentraland.
- **Chat Bubbles**: Messages float as bubbles above the sender's cursor and appear in the chat log, tagged with the district where they were said.
- **Minecraft-Style Chat Commands**: Teleports, a shared day/night cycle, weather, warps, homes, whispers and more (see [Chat commands](#-chat-commands)).
- **Genesis City + Worlds (like Decentraland)**: new developers spawn at **Genesis Plaza**, right in front of the square pool that leads underground. The small maps are scenes scattered organically around the plaza, linked by a ring promenade and winding cobblestone paths with lamps and banners; between them you find gardens, cottages, sculptures, kiosks, campfires with benches, picnic tables, digital art frames that open community links and "for sale" signs. The big maps (Silicon Valley, Museum, Debug Dungeon, Open Source HQ, Keynote Amphitheater, Green-Tech Sandbox) are separate **Worlds** you enter through the side menu, the metro or chat commands (`/world valley`).
- **Underground Genesis Plaza**: step into the **square pool** in the plaza: the submerged doors slide open, you fall 5 seconds down a square water tunnel lit from above, and land in a rock cave with a Welcome Hall, the **Underground Cafe** (bar, stools, espresso), the **Hall of Internet History** (a long red-carpet corridor with lit frames: ARPANET, the first website, BBS, IRC, Usenet, 90s homepages, early browsers...) and, at its end, the **Minimal Art Gallery** (white exposed concrete, very high ceiling, glass walkways over a water mirror, black frames with a floating *View Details* button). An elevator takes you back up.
- **Plaza social spots**: a Decentraland-style **Theatre** whose stage screen plays the WWDC keynotes, a **Firepit** with concentric benches, a **Mini-Game Arena** with jump pads, **Teleport Portals** to the Worlds, a **Panorama Deck** (elevator up to the Hall of Fame and the Newsroom), a **Metro station** and the **Cyber Garage** car showroom.
- **Multiverse Metro**: press **N** (or `/metro`, or E at a station) for a London Underground-style map with four lines (Genesis, Arcade, Leisure, Worlds Express); click a station and ride a train over the landscape.
- **Cyber Garage (in-game vehicles)**: floating hypercars inspired by Italian supercars and electric sports cars (no brand names), hovering over magnetic light strips with color-shifting paint, plus Tron-style light cycles that leave a fading neon trail. E to ride, E again to get off. In-game items only: no NFTs, no blockchain.
- **Tech Square** (far district): Times Square-style LED screens with live data: a GitHub mural of recent commits and Pull Requests (fireworks when a big PR merges), a Big Tech stock scoreboard and dev trending topics (Hacker News, Stack Overflow, Reddit), fetched by the server.
- **Gamer District** (far district): the **Gravity Flip** tower, a translucent skyscraper climbed with trampolines through floor openings, with low-gravity floors and a floating lounge on top that rewards the climb with a rare hoodie.
- **Aero-Glass Oasis** (far district): floating glass villas over a water mirror on neon light beams; smart-glass walls switch between clear, mirrored and frosted (E), and low-gravity infinity pools let you float up to the sunroom.
- **Graphic Equalizer · The Tower of Icons**: an abstract tower with no walls, whose facade is made of real app icons (WhatsApp, Spotify, Instagram, YouTube, Discord, Telegram, Netflix...). Press E at the jukebox: the music starts and the icon columns pulse like the bars of a giant equalizer. Climb the spiral of floating icons: each one you step on plays its own effect (WhatsApp dials, Messenger and Discord pop chat bubbles, Spotify throws music notes, Instagram flashes...).
- **Skyline of Tech**: a ring of skyscrapers around the city carries giant LED billboards with the **real logos** of big tech companies and developer tools (loaded at runtime from [Simple Icons](https://simpleicons.org), CC0), and blimps with banners circle the sky. Red mesas with autumn trees and a sweeping arch fill the horizon; the only clouds are the blocky Minecraft-style layer.
- **A Decentraland-style Genesis Plaza**: at human scale, with warm cobblestone, a round **Welcome Hall** (wooden floor, glass waterfall tube, mezzanine, espresso bar, crystals, cafe tables with laptops), the **GitHub Multiverse Theatre** with its bulb marquee, an **Events Wall** whose cards jump into each World, a **Cozy Pond** with lily pads, a bridge and fishing spots, event posters, shops with awnings, a neon tower, a casino, an arch gate, hot-air balloons and **showcase shelves** of rare hardware, web legends and dev tools.
- **Enterable Headquarters**: every Silicon Valley HQ has a real door, a lobby with the company logo, stairs to every floor and the rooftop, open-plan offices with laptops, a glass meeting room and a coffee corner. The **House of MAC** is an Apple Store with the Mac Finder logo over the glass entrance, Avenue tables with MacBooks, iPhones and iPads to try, a working **Genius Bar** (ask a Genius for Mac and developer tips), a wall of official Apple app icons, an ad carousel, a Today at Apple video wall, a screen playing Apple's own YouTube uploads and outdoor ad billboards (see [House of MAC banners](#-house-of-mac-banners-and-logos)).
- **Roblox-style Actions**: press **E** near objects to work on a laptop (type anything and real-looking code appears, then commit to the Commit Leaderboard), sit on chairs, sofas, stools and lily pads, grab a coffee (speed boost), play **Pac-Git Snake** on arcade cabinets, fish at the pond, inspect showcase items or jump into Worlds. Emotes: `/e wave`, `dance`, `cheer`, `clap`, `sit`, `think`, `ship`, `coffee`.
- **Creative Flight**: press **F** to fly like Minecraft's creative mode (Space up, Shift down, F again to land). Space always jumps right away, like Roblox.
- **Invite Peers & Private Lobbies**: buttons at the top copy an invite link to your current spot or open a **new private lobby** (`?lobby=<id>`): only devs with that link see and chat with you there.
- **Interactive Districts**: a co-op dungeon crawler against famous bugs, an Open Source HQ where Pull Requests change the map, a keynote amphitheater showing Apple's WWDC keynotes, a museum of forgotten tech and a Green-Tech sandbox.
- **Silicon Valley**: one tech campus where the headquarters stand side by side along boulevards around a central plaza: Apple Park, Googleplex, Meta, Anthropic, OpenAI, GitHub, Tesla, Microsoft, Intel, NVIDIA, HP, LinkedIn, Uber, Netflix, Cisco, Oracle, EA, Adobe and eBay.
- **Autoplay video screens**: the keynote screens, the Theatre and the Apple Store play muted automatically when you walk close (browsers only allow muted autoplay; click a screen for sound).
- **Screenshot mode**: **Shift+U** hides the whole interface and leaves only the scenery.
- **Clean, Decentraland-style HUD**: only chat bubbles (they fade after a while; Enter shows the history), a small chat field, your location and coin balance, and a **dock** in the bottom-right corner that opens the profile, places, minimap, metro, Codify, inventory, invites and controls.
- **Codify**: press **C** (or use the dock) to open a code editor on the right. Pick a language, write code and press **Add to world**: a soft spinning terminal block floats in front of you with your code above it, updating live as you type. Blocks are **private** (only you see them) unless you tick **Visible to others**, then the devs in your lobby see the block and its code in real time. E on your block edits it again.
- **Fry your Code**: at the Genesis Plaza **Firepit** (a real campfire with logs, embers, layered flames, sparks and smoke) press E: your code block goes on a stick and roasts over the fire while bugs pop out of it. It is a playful bug finder (unbalanced brackets, `==`, `var`, leftover `console.log`/TODOs, hardcoded secrets, bare `except:`, infinite loops, `DELETE` without `WHERE`, `unwrap()`... plus the browser's own JavaScript parser for syntax errors), with a roast report in the Codify panel.
- **Roblox-style seats everywhere**: E sits on benches, sofas, stools, theatre and amphitheater seats, HQ chairs, campfire benches, picnic tables and more.
- **Spawn hub & Events Explorer**: the spawn faces a large square pool (14 m, reflective rippling water, tiled walls and caustics) inside a game-lobby style hub: low terraces with blue neon trims and water channels, octagonal tree planters, star pads that jump to the Worlds, a card table, a giant Connect Four, a gazebo lounge and canopy tents. Around the pool, ringed by walls of **Top Rated** scenes, **Events** (the Worlds) and live **Dev News**, with JUMP IN cards, a diving board into the pool and a floating golden arrow. Paths start there and lead to every district, and the districts are linked to each other by winding paths.
- **Multiverse Hotel & mini hotels**: an architectural hotel district (giant neon "M" letters around a glass tower, red swirling lobby, spiral stairs, floating beds, teal lounge) plus mini capsule and neon-cube hotels in Genesis City and in several Worlds. **E on a bed at night** sets your spawn point (`/bed`); when half of the online devs are asleep the night is skipped for everyone, like Minecraft.
- **In-game economy (not real money)**: Multiverse Coins (**MVC**) are earned with commits (+3), chests (+5), supply drops (+25) and the Gravity Flip reward (+50), and spent on **land parcels** (250 MVC) marked by "PARCEL FOR SALE" signs around Genesis City; bought parcels show the owner. `/pay` sends coins to another dev. By default MVC live only on the server. Optionally (see [Web3 on testnet](#-web3-on-testnet-optional)) they can be mirrored as an ERC-20 token on a **test network** (Base Sepolia or Polygon Amoy): test tokens have no real value and cannot be bought or sold.
- **Avatar customization**: before entering (and anytime with the 👕 button) dress your cursor like a Decentraland backpack: cursor color, classic pixel "deal with it" shades, round glasses, neon visor or heart shades, a jacket cut to the shape of the arrow (leather, puffer, varsity, hoodie, tuxedo) and a cap, beanie, top hat, crown, headphones or halo. Saved on your account and visible to everyone.
- **Underground Cafe menu**: E at the cafe menu board orders Italian-style "dishes" with Multiverse Coins. Every dish is a real package of code or tools (Carbonara Docker = a production Dockerfile, Risotto di GitHub = a GitHub Actions CI workflow, Gnocchi Kubernetes = a Deployment + Service, Pizza VS Code = editor settings, Panna Cotta Blockchain = an educational hash chain...): it is served on the counter, **downloaded as a file to your computer**, opened in Codify and kept in Codify → My packages. Cocktails (Python Negroni, Aperol Script, Terminal Espresso Martini, AWS Cloud Sour, Linux Mint Julep) also give a short buff.
- **Coin indicator & daily bonus**: the top-right coin shows your MVC; click it for the daily bonus and the ways to earn more.
- **Sound effects (optional)**: the hiss of a mouse gliding on a mousepad while your cursor moves, jump and landing, coins, teleports and crickets at night. Toggle with 🔊 or `/sound`.
- **Timeline Hall (Museum)**: a building inside a building: lit frames from 1969 to 2005 (ARPANET, Windows 1.0, the first website, Mosaic, Windows 95, personal homepages, the late-90s Apple site, Winamp, Google beta, instant messengers, Mac OS 9, the office assistant, the Blue Screen of Death, Thefacebook, YouTube; illustrated recreations) around the white **Memorable Mentions** vault with hardware nobody uses anymore.
- **The Hotel**: a two-floor log cabin (green wallpaper, a stone chimney fireplace, a mezzanine with stairs, rooms with beds, pendant lamps, cobwebs) with a title card when you walk in. Its back door leads to a fenced backyard with pine trees, a concrete bunker and a **hatch** (E): below it, a maze of metal ventilation ducts; follow the light strips to the blue vent and it drops you into the Genesis Plaza Underground. In the Multiverse Hotel district and in Genesis City.
- **Softer, less blocky world**: every box in the world has rounded edges and surfaces get a subtle grain; Genesis City is paved with stone.
- **Multiverse Forest**: gardens enclosed by hedges with a giant tree, a screen counting the trees devs planted (`/plant` in the Green-Tech Sandbox), green-coding tips on boards, flowers and benches.
- **House of MAC flagship**: a two-story stone corner store with LED shop windows showing the Mac ads (`public/assets/ads/`), neon signs and an open black steel frame on top with giant glowing Apple, macOS and Xcode logos, plus a reflective sidewalk with neon lines.
- **GitHub events screen (Tech Square)**: a countdown to the next GitHub event (GitHub Universe 2026, Oct 28–29, San Francisco and online), a LIVE badge while it happens (E opens the official site) and the livestream on the screen once its YouTube link is added to `public/maps/github_events.json`; next to it, replays of past Universe keynotes.

## 🔗 Web3 on testnet (optional)

Everything works without this. To mirror MVC on a test network:

1. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` and fill `THIRDWEB_CLIENT_ID`, `THIRDWEB_SECRET_KEY`, `MVC_CHAIN` (`base-sepolia` or `polygon-amoy`), `MVC_TOKEN_ADDRESS`, `GAME_ADMIN_PRIVATE_KEY` and `GAME_TREASURY_ADDRESS`.
2. Deploy the token from `blockchain/` (`cd blockchain && npm install && npm test`): `contracts/MVCToken.sol` is an OpenZeppelin ERC-20 (capped, burnable) whose `mint` only the owner or the game contract can call.
3. `npm start` builds `public/vendor/web3.js` (thirdweb in-app wallets, gas sponsored). Players log in with email or Google, get a wallet automatically (address shown discreetly), can **claim** their in-game MVC as test tokens and buy land with an on-chain transfer of 250 MVC (verified by the server before the parcel is granted).

Only test networks are accepted; mainnets are refused by the server.

## 🔐 Accounts

The first screen asks you to **create an account** (username + password) or **log in**.

- Usernames: 3-16 letters, numbers or `_` (case-insensitive and unique). Passwords: at least 8 characters.
- The server stores accounts in `data/users.json` (ignored by git): passwords are hashed with `scrypt` and a random salt, never stored in plain text. Logins and sign-ups are rate-limited per IP.
- After signing in you get a session token (valid for 30 days) saved in your browser, so the next visit skips the screen. **Log out** is in the top-left panel.
- Your username is your in-world name, and your loot inventory is saved with your account.
- In production, always serve the site over **HTTPS** so passwords are encrypted in transit.
- On static hosts without the server (solo mode) the account is saved only in that browser, as the welcome screen warns.

The welcome screen shows the GitHub Multiverse logo over the README banner. For the most reliable result, save the banner image as `public/assets/banner.png`; if that file does not exist, the screen loads the original image from GitHub.

## 🎁 Loot

Golden **chests** sit on street corners all over Genesis City and next to the Silicon Valley headquarters. Walk up to one and press **E**: the server rolls an item, and the chest respawns 2 minutes later. Every 3 minutes a **supply drop** falls from the sky under a balloon, with a blue beam of light visible from far away and a chat announcement; it holds Rare to Legendary loot for whoever opens it first. Epic and Legendary finds are announced to everyone.

| Rarity | Chest odds | Supply drop odds | Items |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Common (grey) | 45% | - | ☕ Espresso Shot (run faster 20s), 🧢 Hackathon Cap, ✅ Green CI Badge (confetti), 🗒️ Sticky Note of Wisdom (dev tip) |
| Uncommon (green) | 30% | - | ⚡ Energy Drink (jump higher 20s), 🍵 Mug of Infinite Coffee, 💡 Accepted Stack Overflow Answer |
| Rare (blue) | 15% | 45% | 🦆 Rubber Duck Debugger (a pet that follows you), 🎧 Noise-Cancelling Headphones, 🔨 Git Commit Hammer, 🌀 Teleport Token |
| Epic (purple) | 8% | 40% | 🧥 Senior Dev Hoodie, 🔍 Debugger Lens (double damage vs bugs 60s), ⏪ git revert Scroll (undo your last teleport) |
| Legendary (gold) | 2% | 15% | 🚀 Jetpack.js (hold Space to fly 30s), 👑 10x Engineer Crown |

Wearables show on your cursor for everyone. Consumables are used up; wearables, the duck and the hammer can be equipped any time. The inventory holds 20 items. The catalog, odds and chest spots live in `public/maps/loot.json`.

## 🗺️ Maps

Every map is a JSON file in `public/maps/` and is registered in `public/maps/worlds.json`: small maps get a `cell` on the Genesis City grid, big maps are marked `"world": true` and get their own far-away `region`. Each `type` in a map is turned into the object it describes (a palm tree, an arcade cabinet, a Kanban board...) by the builders in `public/props.js`, `landmarks.js`, `museum.js` and `games.js`.

## 🧱 Adding a map

1. Create `public/maps/map_<name>.json` (the existing district maps are bundled in `public/maps/districts.json` under their file names; a new map can go in its own file or as a new entry in that bundle):
   ```json
   {
     "themeName": "My District",
     "backgroundColor": "#1e1e24",
     "blocks": [
       { "type": "floor", "position": [0, -0.5, 0], "scale": [40, 1, 40], "color": "#2d2d34" },
       { "type": "kanban_board_todo", "position": [0, 3, -10], "scale": [6, 5, 0.3], "color": "#ffffff", "label": "Kanban: To Do" }
     ]
   }
   ```
   `position` and `scale` are `[x, y, z]` in meters relative to the district center. `label` shows on hover and in the info card.
2. Register it in `public/maps/worlds.json`. A small scene takes a free `cell` on the city grid (districts are built 1.5× the 64 m cell, so a cell district is about 88 m wide), or an exact `region` anywhere on the parcel grid:
   ```json
   { "key": "mydistrict", "file": "map_my_district.json", "name": "My District", "description": "...", "cell": [-3, 2], "color": "#ff2d7a" }
   ```
   A big map becomes a separate World with its own island, far from the city:
   ```json
   { "key": "myworld", "file": "map_my_world.json", "name": "My World", "description": "...", "world": true, "region": { "center": [17000, 0], "size": [120, 120] }, "color": "#3a86ff" }
   ```

The block `type` decides the model. Some of the recognized types: `floor`, `wall`, `road`, `water`, `desk`, `table`, `sofa`, `stairs_step`, `kanban_board_*`, `sticky_note`, `palm_trunk`, `palm_leaves`, `arcade_cabinet_*`, `pool_table_base`, `server_rack`, `neon_pillar`, `stage`, `pedestal`, `plant`, `billboard`, `hq_<company>`, and many more (see the rule lists at the end of each builder file). Unknown types fall back to an outlined block.

## ☁️ Deploying

Multiplayer needs the Node server running on a host that keeps a Node process alive and supports WebSockets (e.g. Render, Railway or Fly.io). Static hosts such as Vercel only serve the `public/` folder: the universe still loads, but in **solo mode** (no other devs; mini-game state stays local).

Accounts and inventories are saved in `data/users.json`; give the host a persistent disk (or volume) so they survive redeploys. The rest of the game state (leaderboard, murals, Pull Requests, planted trees, live stream, chests) lives in the server's memory and resets when the server restarts.

Optional environment variables:

| Variable | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `PORT` | Server port (default 3000) |
| `SUPPLY_DROP_EVERY_MS` | Time between supply drops (default 180000, 3 minutes) |
| `GITHUB_TOKEN` | GitHub token for the Tech Square commits/PRs mural (works without it, with GitHub's lower rate limit) |
| `FINNHUB_API_KEY` | Free [Finnhub](https://finnhub.io) key for the stock scoreboard. Without it the scoreboard says it is not configured: prices are never invented |
| `DISABLE_FEEDS=1` | Turn off all live feeds (offline / tests) |

## ⚡ Performance

The world has more than ten thousand objects, so the game only draws what is near you:

- **Distance culling:** buildings are skipped only where the fog already hides them completely (the view never changes), and small details (benches, lamps, plants) are skipped beyond 70–170 m depending on the quality level.
- **Automatic quality:** the game watches the frame rate and, when it gets slow, lowers the resolution, turns off shadows and draws small details closer; it goes back up when there is room. Use `/quality low | medium | high | auto` to choose (or `minimum` on very old computers); the choice is remembered in the browser.
- **Steady walking speed:** slow frames are split into small physics steps, so you walk at the same speed at 20 or 60 FPS.
- **Lighter scene:** no point lights (fires and lamps glow on their own), shadows only from large objects, and lighter rounded boxes.

The server lists the files in these folders (`GET /api/owner-assets`), so the game only requests images that exist. Billboards resize to each banner's aspect ratio. Add more banners to the carousel by listing them in `public/assets/ads/ads.json`. Until a file exists, the store shows the official Simple Icons logos and generated boards. Apple ads and logos are copyrighted / trademarks of Apple Inc.: only publish them if you have the right to.
